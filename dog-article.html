ChatGPT's humble dog sketch 'sells for $8.7 MILLION' in imagined auction that sets the internet barking

A simple pencil portrait called Man's Best Algorithm becomes the unlikely centrepiece of an $8.7 million art-world frenzy, in a satirical thought experiment about AI and creativity.

26 October 2026 · Arts and technology · Creative fiction

Editor's note: This is a fictional story created for an unofficial website mockup. No auction, $8.7 million sale, or buyer has been independently verified, and none should be understood as real.

It began with the kind of request that millions of people make to artificial intelligence every day: draw me a dog. The result was a modest pencil sketch, a quiet portrait of a loyal companion, framed in gold.

In the imagined auction, that unassuming drawing ends up selling for an extraordinary $8.7 million. The sketch, titled Man's Best Algorithm, suddenly forces collectors and artists alike to consider a question that sounds ridiculous until the price tag comes into view: what makes art valuable when the maker is software?

No years of formal training, no solitary nights in a studio and no painter's personal history lie behind the image. Instead, the story centres on a generated illustration that looks almost old-fashioned.

What is a collector actually buying?

The original dog's unshowy expression and delicate shading give the image its charm. But in this fictional scenario, the enormous price reflects the story surrounding the work rather than any rare technique. The painting becomes a cultural object, a conversation about authorship, novelty and the strange economics of attention.

The title plays on the phrase man's best friend, blending old-fashioned affection for dogs with an uncomfortable new relationship: humanity and the algorithms it builds.

For all the imaginary millions, the central joke is simple. A machine draws a dog, the world argues about whether it is art, and an anonymous buyer walks away with the most expensive good boy in the room.
