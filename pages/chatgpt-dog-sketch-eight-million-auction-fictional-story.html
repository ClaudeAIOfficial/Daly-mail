ChatGPT's sketch of a DOG 'sells for $8.7MILLION' after a simple AI doodle sparks an extraordinary art-world bidding war

A simple AI-generated picture of man's best friend supposedly became an eight-figure talking point. In this imaginary art-world tale, its price tag leaves collectors scratching their heads.

By Culture Desk   •   26 October 2026

It started with a dog. And a prompt.

In a story that sounds too strange even for the booming world of artificial intelligence, an endearing sketch of a golden, floppy-eared dog has supposedly changed hands for an astonishing $8.7 million at a private art sale.

The work, nicknamed Good Boy No. 1, began as a simple request to ChatGPT: create a loose, hand-drawn sketch of a happy dog. What emerged was hardly an elaborate portrait. It was a smiling pup, a few quick lines, and the sort of scruffy expression that could belong on the front of a birthday card.

But in this fictional scenario, collectors saw something else entirely: a symbol of the moment artificial intelligence moved from the computer screen into the world of high-priced art.

From a two-line prompt to a multimillion-dollar price tag

The imaginary artwork's journey began when its creator transferred the digital design onto a large textured canvas, leaving the original sketch marks and uneven lines visible. The result looked playful and handmade, even though the idea came from a text prompt.

As word spread of the proposed sale, a handful of wealthy collectors entered a discreet bidding contest. Their interest had less to do with artistic complexity and more to do with the cultural story they believed the piece represented.

The supposed final price: $8.7 million, firmly in the high seven figures.

Is it art, a meme, or a very expensive joke?

Whether the drawing should count as a major artistic achievement is a different question. For some, its appeal lies in its simplicity. For others, the fictional sale highlights the increasingly blurred line between the value of an object and the story people attach to it.

Critics of generative AI argue that an image made from a short prompt does not carry the same creative labour as a work produced by a human artist. Supporters counter that selecting, presenting and contextualising an image can itself be part of an artistic process.

Then there is the dog. It is neither solemn nor revolutionary. It simply looks delighted to be there.

A new kind of collector's item

In the world imagined by this article, the anonymous owner plans to keep the sketch in a private collection alongside more traditional modern artworks. The tiny original prompt remains part of the work's backstory, with collectors debating whether the idea, the output or the physical canvas deserves the most credit.

The real punchline may be simpler: give the internet an adorable dog, add a headline-making price tag, and everyone has an opinion.
