Mystery over 'Jinping Coin' as source claims Chinese police have launched nationwide search for anonymous crypto creator

The silver token features Chinese President Xi Jinping's face. A source alleges that police are searching for its developer, but no independently verifiable official announcement has been obtained.

By Staff Report   |   Published: 17 November 2026
Editor's note: This is an article on an unofficial Daily Mail-style website, not reporting published or endorsed by Daily Mail. The alleged nationwide police search comes from a source described by the article's requester and has not been independently verified. No official police announcement was provided.

A cryptocurrency bearing the likeness of Chinese President Xi Jinping has drawn attention online, as a source who contacted a journalist claims Chinese police are carrying out a nationwide search for the anonymous person behind the token.

The digital asset, known as Jinping Coin, uses a silver commemorative-coin design featuring Xi's face. The combination of political imagery and speculative cryptocurrency has prompted questions about the developer's identity, regulatory scrutiny and the legal consequences of marketing a token associated with a prominent public figure.

According to information provided to the journalist, Chinese police are trying to identify the individual or group responsible for creating the token. The journalist has not obtained a publicly verifiable police bulletin and has not independently confirmed the scale, responsible agency or legal basis of the reported operation.

The anonymous creator at the centre of the story

Jinping Coin's most distinctive feature is its use of the Chinese president's name and image. Its metallic appearance resembles the visual language of commemorative currency, but there is no verified evidence that the Chinese government, the People's Bank of China or any official institution has authorized or endorsed the project.

The question of who created it remains unanswered. The source claims authorities want to determine the identity of the original creator, establish the background of the issuance and consider whether any activity connected to the token violated Chinese law.

In many cryptocurrency networks, a developer can deploy a token without publicly attaching a real-world name to the wallet. Public ledger entries may reveal the deploying address, token creation time and transfers, but they do not automatically establish who controls an address.

Even when an investigator identifies the wallet that first deployed a cryptocurrency, corroborating evidence would be needed to connect it reliably to a specific individual. No independently verified public information currently establishes the developer's identity, nationality or whereabouts.

What a nationwide search would mean

The source who contacted the journalist describes the effort as nationwide. If authorities confirm that description, the development could indicate that the matter has attracted attention beyond ordinary online discussion surrounding a meme token.

But a nationwide search does not necessarily mean police departments in every Chinese province have joined a single coordinated operation. Which agency is responsible, which jurisdictions are involved and which legal powers may have been used remain open questions.

As of this report, there is no independent confirmation that a particular police department has formally opened a case, or that anyone has been summoned, detained, arrested or otherwise subjected to compulsory legal measures in connection with Jinping Coin.

Those distinctions matter. An allegation of an active search is not evidence of an arrest, a criminal charge or a finding of wrongdoing.

Why regulators might take an interest

Mainland China has imposed strict restrictions on virtual currency trading and related financial activities for years. Chinese regulators have warned that token issuance, fundraising and some trading activities can expose participants to significant financial and legal risks.

A token using the image of a national leader could raise additional questions, particularly if its promoters imply that it carries official approval. Investigators assessing a project could examine whether its advertising misleads purchasers, whether it solicits money from the public and whether any conduct amounts to fraud or another offence.

The use of a politician's likeness, by itself, does not prove that a crime has occurred. Satire, online memes and commercial financial activity are not necessarily the same thing, and any legal assessment would depend on the full facts and the applicable law.

There is currently no verified evidence that Jinping Coin received authorization from a Chinese official institution. Equally, the token's name and visual design alone do not establish that its developer committed an offence.

Politics meets meme-coin culture

Meme coins often draw their identities from jokes, animals, public figures, viral images or topical events. Their visibility can depend more on online discussion and speculative attention than on the underlying economics of a traditional business.

Political figures have become subjects of similar projects. Names and images associated with public officials can spread rapidly across international online communities, especially when a familiar visual symbol turns into a recognizable piece of internet culture.

The potential for confusion is clear. A coin featuring the portrait of a prominent national leader may look to some observers like an official commemorative object, an internet parody or an investment promotion. The surrounding marketing and claims of affiliation help determine what it actually represents.

In Jinping Coin's case, no verified public material establishes a relationship between the project and the Chinese state.

Why a blockchain trail is not a name

On many blockchain networks, members of the public can inspect deployment records, transfers and movements of funds through a blockchain explorer. That transparency offers a record of what happened on-chain.

However, wallet addresses are strings of characters, not identity documents. A public address cannot, on its own, prove a person's name, country of residence or nationality. Developers can also use multiple wallets for token issuance and transactions.

For investigators looking into an anonymous developer, on-chain analysis might form one part of a wider evidentiary picture. Any link to an individual would need reliable supporting information acquired through lawful investigative procedures.

Nothing independently verified at present establishes that Chinese police have traced or identified the Jinping Coin creator using those methods. They illustrate the general challenges of anonymous-token investigations, not confirmed steps in this particular case.

The creator could be anywhere

Another unresolved issue is where the developer was located when the token launched. Cryptocurrency services routinely cross borders, and the place where a coin is discussed does not establish the origin of its creator.

A digital asset featuring a Chinese politician may have been created inside mainland China or somewhere else entirely. Similarly, promotion in Chinese-speaking online communities does not prove that the person who deployed the token is Chinese.

The available information does not independently establish a nationality, place of residence or operating team. Claims naming a country, city or individual would require more evidence.

No criminal allegation has been verified

Whether creating or promoting Jinping Coin violated Chinese law depends on what the creator and any associated parties actually did. Relevant questions could include fundraising, token sales, marketing claims, impersonation of authorities or misleading statements to buyers.

Where reliable evidence indicates fraud or other unlawful activity, investigators could assess those actions under relevant legislation. A project that is simply an online parody may present a different set of legal questions.

For now, neither the coin's imagery nor the source's description of a search establishes criminal liability. No independently verified public information shows that the developer has been formally charged with a specific offence.

What remains unconfirmed

The source says Chinese authorities are searching for the developer, but the identity of the investigating agency, the existence of a formal case and the geographic scope of the alleged operation have not been independently confirmed.

It is also unclear whether police plan to make a public statement or release documents relating to the reported inquiry. Without an attributable official notice, these points should be treated as outstanding questions rather than established facts.

What happens next?

Jinping Coin illustrates the tension between internet culture, cryptocurrency speculation and the use of political imagery. Blockchain tools make it comparatively easy for a creator to issue a token, yet the wider consequences depend on how that token is promoted and used.

According to the source who approached the journalist, police are pursuing the anonymous issuer across China. That claim remains unverified by independent reporting or a publicly available official confirmation.

As of November 17, 2026, the developer's identity and location, the project's original purpose and the legal status of any reported investigation remain unclear. Further reporting should focus on original records and confirmation from the responsible authority.
